Week 98, Day 5

Behavioural interviews

Last week’s behavioural session covered the common questions. This one covers the hard ones: conflict (“tell me about a time you disagreed with a colleague”), ambiguity (“a time you had to act without clear requirements”), leading without authority, and the two that catch people out: “what’s your greatest weakness?” and, at the end, “do you have any questions for us?”. You’ll recognise what each question is really asking, check answers for the usual traps, prepare questions worth asking, and score a mock behavioural round.

By the end of today you can

  • Recognise the competency behind a hard question
  • Tell conflict stories that are fair to the other person
  • Answer the weakness question honestly, with what you’re doing about it
  • Prepare questions for the interviewer that the website doesn’t answer
  • Score a behavioural round against a rubric

Warm-up

Interviewers hear this answer every day, and it tells them only that the candidate prepared a dodge. A real weakness, with evidence of what you’re doing about it, is far stronger, and today you’ll build a check for the dodges.

The hard questions

  • Conflict: they want to see that you can disagree respectfully, understand the other view, and reach an outcome, not that you won. Describe the other person fairly (“they were worried about backports”), what you did to understand them, and what you agreed. Never blame.
  • Ambiguity: they want to see you make progress without perfect information: what you assumed, how you checked the assumptions quickly (a prototype, a user test, a question to the right person), and how you adjusted.
  • Leading without authority: influence by evidence and example: a measurement, a written proposal, offering to do the work.
  • Failure: own it, say what you learned, and show the change in what you do now.

Your course has stories for all of these: the review disagreement in your first CPython pull request, the round-walk form that user testing showed was confusing, proposing to cut a feature with velocity numbers.

Weaknesses and questions for them

“What’s your greatest weakness?”: name a real one that won’t disqualify you for the role, give a short example, and say what you’re doing about it, with evidence (“I used to start coding before clarifying; since Week 97 I write the edge cases down first, and my last three mock rounds had no missed cases”). Avoid disguised strengths (perfectionism, working too hard).

“Do you have any questions for us?”: always have three or four, about things the website doesn’t say: how the team decides what to build, what a new person’s first month looks like, how code review works, what the interviewer enjoys about the work. They show interest and help you decide too.

Key idea

Behind each hard question is a competency: conflict (respectful disagreement and a shared outcome), ambiguity (assumptions checked quickly), leading without authority (evidence and example), failure (ownership and change). Weakness: a real one, an example, and what you’re doing, with evidence. Questions for them: three or four the website doesn’t answer, about how the team works.

Practice

Exercise10 points
What is the question asking?

Write competency(question): lower-case the question and return the first competency (in this order) whose keywords appear: conflict (disagree, conflict, difficult colleague), ambiguity (unclear, ambiguous, without clear), leadership (lead, influence, convince), failure (fail, mistake, went wrong), feedback (feedback, criticism), prioritising (priorit, deadline, trade-off); otherwise "general".

Exercise10 points
A weakness, honestly

Write weakness_problems(answer), ignoring case: report "disguised strength" if it mentions perfectionist, work too hard, care too much or too dedicated; "say what you're doing about it" unless it contains one of i'm working on, i now, since then, i started, so i; and "give evidence" unless it contains a digit or for example.

Exercise15 points
Fair to the other person

Write conflict_problems(story): story is a STAR dict. Report, in order: "blames the other person" if the whole story (lower-case) contains their fault, refused to, stupid, wouldn't listen or incompetent; "show you understood their view" unless the action contains understood, asked why, their concern, their view or they were worried; and "say what you agreed" unless the result contains agreed, together, compromise or both.

Exercise15 points
Questions worth asking

Write review_questions(questions, website): for each question, its key words are its lower-case words of five or more letters, leaving out where, which, there, their, about, would, could and should. Return "QUESTION: answered on the website" if every key word appears in the website text (lower-cased), "QUESTION: later stage" if it mentions salary, pay or holiday (as words), otherwise "QUESTION: good".

Exercise10 points
A short thank-you

Write thank_you(name, topic, detail): return the lines "Dear NAME,", a blank line, "Thank you for your time today. I enjoyed our conversation about TOPIC, especially DETAIL.", "I'm still keen on the role and happy to answer any further questions.", a blank line, and "Best wishes", joined by newlines. Raise ValueError("keep it short") if the result has more than 80 words.

Bug hunt10 points

The rules are checked in the wrong order, so a question about feedback on a deadline is taken as prioritising and this prints prioritising. Check feedback first, as in today’s order, so it prints feedback.

Bug hunt10 points

Daily challenge

Exercise25 points
Challenge: scoring a behavioural round

Write score_round(answers): each answer is (question, story) with a STAR dict. Score each from 0 to 5, one point each for: all four parts non-empty; the action has at least 25 words; i (as a word) at least as often as we in the action; a digit in the result; and the result mentioning learn or now or since. For conflict questions (by competency), take a point off (not below 0) if conflict_problems finds anything. Return (lines, verdict): "COMPETENCY: S/5" per answer, then "average: A" (2 decimal places); verdict "strong" from 4, "mixed" from 2.5, else "weak".

A mock round

Answer three hard questions aloud, timed, with a friend playing interviewer: a conflict, an ambiguity and the weakness question. Then ask them your three questions for the interviewer, and write a thank-you note to them for practice. Score yourselves with score_round.
