Week 92, Day 3

Demo video

Most people who meet Waymark will meet it as a two-minute video before they ever install it. A good demo shows the problem, then the product solving it, then stops; it’s planned like a small piece of software, with a script that’s timed before recording, captions so it works with the sound off (as most videos are watched) and for people who can’t hear it, and chapters so viewers can jump to the part they care about. Today you’ll write the SRT caption format and read it back, time a script from its word count, check captions against the usual guidelines, and generate chapters. The challenge checks a whole demo plan before anything is recorded.

By the end of today you can

  • Plan a short demo around the problem and a few real tasks
  • Time a script from its words before recording
  • Write and read SRT caption files
  • Check captions for overlaps, long lines and reading speed
  • Generate chapter markers from the script

Warm-up

2.3 isn’t exactly 2.3 in binary, so seconds % 1 is a hair under 0.3 and int cuts it to 299 milliseconds. Caption times are written to the millisecond, so they need rounding, not cutting.

Planning the demo

Two to three minutes is enough. Structure it like the write-up, but shown:

  1. The problem, in one sentence and one picture: a walker surprised by a climb.
  2. Three tasks, the ones users tested on Day 5 (plan a walk, find a round walk, share it), each done for real, at normal speed, with real data.
  3. A close: what it is, that it’s free, and where to find it.

Write the narration as a script first, one scene per task, with a time budget for each. Prepare the screen: a clean browser profile or terminal, large text (zoom to 150%), notifications off, sample data loaded, nothing private visible.

Timing the script

People speak about 150 words a minute when explaining something clearly. So a scene’s length can be estimated before recording: words divided by 150, times 60 seconds. A 63-word scene takes about 25 seconds; if its budget is 20, cut words now rather than talking faster later.

Captions

SRT is the simplest caption format, accepted almost everywhere. Each caption is a block: its number, a time range, and one or two lines of text, with a blank line between blocks:

1
00:00:00,000 --> 00:00:03,500
Waymark plans walks with honest times,

2
00:00:03,500 --> 00:00:06,000
counting the climbing as well as the distance.

Times are HH:MM:SS,mmm, with a comma before the milliseconds. Common guidelines (the BBC’s subtitle guidelines are a good free reference) say: at most two lines, about 42 characters a line, on screen for at least a second, no overlaps, and no more than about 17 characters a second of reading.

Chapters and recording

Video sites turn lines like 0:00 Intro in a description into chapters. YouTube’s rules: the first must start at 0:00, there must be at least three, and each must be at least 10 seconds long. Generate them from the script’s timings, then adjust after recording.

Record with a free tool such as OBS Studio, in short takes (one per scene) so a mistake costs one scene; then cut, add the captions file, and watch it once with the sound off.

Key idea

Demo the problem, three real tasks and a close, in two to three minutes, from a timed script (about 150 words a minute). Prepare a clean, large-text screen. Write SRT captions (HH:MM:SS,mmm with a comma, rounded not cut) of at most 42 characters a line, at least a second each, without overlaps, at no more than about 17 characters a second. Generate chapters from 0:00, at least three, each at least 10 seconds. Record scene by scene.

Practice

Exercise15 points
Writing SRT

Write srt_time(seconds) returning HH:MM:SS,mmm, rounding to the nearest millisecond; then to_srt(captions): captions is a list of (start, end, text) with times in seconds; return SRT blocks (number from 1, the range START --> END, the text) separated by blank lines, ending with a newline.

Exercise10 points
Reading SRT

Write parse_srt(text): return a list of (start, end, text) with times as seconds (floats) and a caption’s lines joined by "\n". Blocks are separated by blank lines; allow extra blank lines and a missing final newline.

Exercise10 points
Timing the script

Write script_timing(scenes, wpm=150): each scene is (name, narration, budget_seconds). A scene’s estimate is its number of words (split on whitespace) divided by wpm, times 60, rounded to 1 decimal place. Return one line per scene, "NAME: X s" with X in the g format, adding " (over by Y s)" (Y to 1 decimal place) when it’s over its budget, then "total: T s".

Exercise10 points
Chapters

Write chapters(items): each item is (title, start_seconds). Raise ValueError with "need at least 3 chapters", "first chapter must start at 0:00", or "chapter TITLE is shorter than 10 s" (for the first chapter, in order, whose next chapter starts less than 10 s later), checked in that order. Return lines "M:SS TITLE" (or "H:MM:SS TITLE" from an hour), with start times rounded down to whole seconds.

Exercise15 points
Checking captions

Write caption_problems(captions): for each caption (start, end, text), numbered from 1, report in this order: "N: overlaps the previous caption" if it starts before the previous one ends; "N: shorter than 1 s"; "N: line over 42 characters" if any of its lines is longer; and "N: too fast (X characters a second)" (X to 1 decimal place) if its characters (not counting line breaks) divided by its duration exceed 17.

Bug hunt10 points
A millisecond short

Cutting the fraction of a second with int turns 2.3 s into 00:00:02,299. Round to the nearest millisecond instead, so it prints 00:00:02,300.

Bug hunt10 points
A chapter at 1:5

The seconds aren’t padded, so the chapter 65 seconds in prints as 1:5 Round walks, which video sites don’t recognise. Pad them so it prints 1:05 Round walks.

Puzzle

Daily challenge

Exercise25 points
Challenge: checking the demo plan

Write demo_plan(scenes, captions, limit=180, wpm=150) with today’s rules. Return (lines, verdict):

  • the scene lines exactly as script_timing writes them (without its total);
  • "total: T s of LIMIT s", with " (over)" if T is more than the limit;
  • "captions: ok", or "captions: N problems" followed by each problem as its own line, indented by two spaces;
  • "chapters:" followed by one line per scene, indented by two spaces, "M:SS NAME", each starting at the sum of the earlier scenes’ estimates, rounded down.

verdict is "ready to record" if no scene is over its budget, the total is within the limit and the captions are ok; otherwise "fix: " and the failing parts (scenes, length, captions) joined by ", ".

Your capstone today

  1. Write and time the script: the problem, three tasks, a close; two to three minutes in all.
  2. Prepare the screen and record scene by scene (OBS Studio is free).
  3. Caption it: write the SRT from the script, check it with caption_problems, and fix the timing against the recording.
  4. Add chapters to the description, watch it once with the sound off, and link it from the README.
