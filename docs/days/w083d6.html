Week 83, Day 6Forge day

Forge

This week turned Python into a tool for data: NumPy arrays and their statistics, pandas for selecting, grouping, joining and reshaping tables, matplotlib for charts that tell the truth, and a full cleaning pipeline for a messy file. Today’s Forge is a complete analysis of a week of trips from a city’s bike-hire scheme: load and check the trips, reject the impossible ones, work out the fares, answer the questions the scheme’s planners ask (when is it busiest, who rides for how long, which routes are popular, which stations run out of bikes), and draw the charts for their report. The bonus simulates the docks minute by minute, finds the trips that failed for want of a bike, and plans the van runs that move bikes back to where they’re needed.

By the end of today you can

  • Load timestamped data, derive durations, hours and days, and reject impossible rows with reasons
  • Compute prices with vectorised rules using np.where and np.select
  • Answer planning questions with groupby, value_counts, merge and reindex
  • Draw a two-chart figure for a report
  • Simulate a system from its events, and plan corrections greedily

Forge: a bike-hire analysis

The scheme has six docking stations (in stations.csv, with the area each is in and its number of docks) and a week of trips (in trips.csv: an id, start and end times, the two stations, whether the rider is a member or casual, and whether the bike was classic or electric). Fares are:

Rider Classic Electric
casual £1.50 to unlock, then £0.10 a minute £1.50 to unlock, then £0.20 a minute
member first 30 minutes free, then £0.05 a minute first 30 minutes free, then £0.10 a minute

Write these functions:

  • load_trips(path): read the trips and parse start and end (format "%Y-%m-%d %H:%M"); add minutes (whole minutes from start to end, as integers; negative if the end is before the start), hour (of the start) and day (the start’s day name).
  • split_valid(trips, max_minutes=180): return (valid, rejected): valid is the trips that end after or when they start and last at most max_minutes (index renumbered); rejected is a sorted list of (trip_id, reason) with reasons "ends before it starts" and "too long".
  • fare(rider, bike, minutes): one trip’s fare, rounded to 2 places.
  • add_fares(trips): a new DataFrame with a fare column, computed without a loop (np.select or np.where), equal to fare for every row.
  • summary(trips, stations) for valid trips: a dict with trips (how many), busiest_hour (the start hour with most trips; ties go to the earliest), median_minutes (a float), by_rider (a dict from rider type to {"trips", "avg_minutes", "revenue"}, the last two rounded to 2 places), top_routes (the three most common ((start, end), count) pairs, ties by station names), revenue_by_area (by the start station’s area, 2 places) and net_flow (for every station in stations, sorted by name: arrivals minus departures, as integers).
  • charts(trips): a figure with two axes side by side: on the left, a bar chart of the number of trips starting in each hour from 0 to 23 (24 bars, including empty hours), titled "Trips by hour"; on the right, a histogram of minutes with bins [0, 10, 20, 30, 45, 60, 90, 180], titled "Trip length". Label both axes of both charts.

What the numbers say

Read your summary as a planner would. Castle loses nine bikes over the week and University gains nine: the commute runs one way, so without vans Castle empties every morning. Members take most trips but pay little (their rides are mostly under 30 minutes, which is what membership is for), so most fare revenue comes from casual riders in the Centre. The hour chart has two peaks: what does that suggest about who the riders are? And which of this week’s techniques would you reach for first if the file had a year of trips and a million rows?

Forge bonus: docks and vans

A station can only lend a bike it has, and only take one back into a free dock. Running the week’s trips in time order against real dock counts shows where the scheme fails, and a simple greedy plan says how to fix it overnight.

Exercise30 points
Forge bonus: docks and vans

Write simulate(trips, bikes, docks): bikes and docks map stations to counts. Turn every trip into a departure at its start and an arrival at its end, and process them in time order (at equal times, arrivals first, then by trip id). A departure from a station with no bikes fails: record the trip id in no_bike, and that trip never arrives. An arrival at a full station (bikes equal to docks) is recorded in full_dock, and the bike goes instead to the station with the most free docks (ties by name). Return {"bikes": ..., "no_bike": [...], "full_dock": [...]} (lists in the order the failures happened) without changing the dicts passed in. Then rebalance(bikes, docks, capacity, share=0.5): each station’s target is int(docks × share) bikes; while some station is above target and another below, move bikes from the station furthest above target to the one furthest below (ties by name), at most capacity per van run and no more than either needs; return the list of (from, to, count) runs.
