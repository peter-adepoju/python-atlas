Week 92, Day 5

Presentation

Capstones end with a talk: ten to fifteen minutes to show what you built and what you learned, then questions. A good technical talk is planned like the demo and rehearsed like a performance: a structure, a time budget per section, slides that support you rather than repeat you, and answers ready for the questions you can predict. Today you’ll check slides against simple rules, share out the minutes fairly, summarise rehearsals, measure your pace and filler words from a transcript, and prepare answers. The challenge decides whether a talk is ready or needs another rehearsal.

By the end of today you can

  • Structure a ten to fifteen minute talk about a project
  • Keep slides light, with speaker notes
  • Share a time budget between sections exactly
  • Rehearse with timings and measure pace and filler words
  • Prepare for predictable questions

Warm-up

Rounding each share on its own gives 3, 3 and 3, which loses a minute. Sharing a fixed total in whole numbers needs a method that makes the parts add up, and you’ll write one today.

The shape of the talk

For a 12-minute capstone talk:

  1. Opening (1 minute): who you are, what Waymark is, in a sentence.
  2. The problem (1 to 2 minutes): a person and a situation. A walker on a hill an hour later than planned is more memorable than “time estimation”.
  3. Demo (3 minutes): the demo video’s three tasks, live if you’re confident, or the video if not. Have the video ready either way.
  4. How it works (2 to 3 minutes): the two or three ideas that matter, with one diagram, not a tour of the code.
  5. Results (2 minutes): the numbers from the write-up: budgets, tests, users.
  6. Close (1 minute): what you learned, what’s next, thank you.

Leave time for questions inside the slot, not after it.

Slides

Slides are for the audience’s eyes while you talk; they aren’t your script. A useful rule: a title on every slide, no more than six bullets, and no more than about 40 words, with a diagram or screenshot wherever one can replace words. Put what you want to say in the speaker notes. Use large type (at least 24 point), high contrast, and nothing that relies on colour alone. Number your slides, so questions can refer to them.

Rehearsing

Rehearse out loud, standing up, timed, at least three times, and time each section. Use the median of your rehearsals (Day 1 of last week again): one bad run shouldn’t scare you, and one good one shouldn’t reassure you. Most people run long in the demo and short in the close.

Record one rehearsal and transcribe it (free speech-to-text tools do this). From the transcript you can measure your pace (about 120 to 170 words a minute is comfortable to follow) and your filler words (“um”, “uh”, “basically”, “you know”). A few are natural; several a minute distract. A pause is better than a filler.

Questions

Most questions are predictable: why this approach and not another, what happens when something goes wrong, how you know it works, what you’d do differently, what was hardest. Write a short answer for each, pointing at a slide or a number where you can. When you don’t know: say so, say how you’d find out, and offer to follow up. Repeat each question before answering, so the whole room (and any recording) hears it.

Key idea

Shape the talk: opening, problem, demo, how it works, results, close, with questions inside the slot. Slides: a title, at most six bullets and about 40 words, notes for what you’ll say, large type. Share the minutes so they add up. Rehearse aloud at least three times, take the median per section, and aim for 120 to 170 words a minute with few fillers. Prepare answers for the predictable questions; repeat each question; say when you don’t know.

Practice

Exercise10 points
Checking the slides

Write slide_problems(slides, max_bullets=6, max_words=40): each slide is a dict with title, bullets (a list) and notes. For each slide, numbered from 1, report in this order: "slide N has no title" (missing or empty), "slide N has B bullets (max M)", "slide N has W words (max M)" (words in the title and bullets, split on whitespace), and "slide N has no speaker notes".

Exercise15 points
Minutes that add up

Write allocate(weights, total): weights maps each section to a weight. Give each section the whole number part of its exact share (weight / sum of weights * total), then hand out the minutes still left, one each, to the sections with the largest fractional parts (ties in the order given). Return a dict in the original order. Use fractions.Fraction so the arithmetic is exact.

Exercise15 points
Rehearsal timings

Write rehearsal_summary(runs, plan): runs is a list of dicts from section to seconds (one per rehearsal); plan maps each section to its planned seconds. Return, for each section in plan’s order, "SECTION: M s (plan P s, D s)" where M is the median of the runs (in the g format) and D the difference written with a sign (+2, -5, +0, in the +g format); then "total: T s (plan P s)" with T the sum of the medians.

Exercise10 points
Pace and filler words

Write speech_stats(transcript, seconds): lower-case the transcript; words are runs of letters and apostrophes. Count each of the fillers um, uh, er, you know, basically and sort of as whole words or phrases. Return a dict with words, wpm (words a minute, rounded to a whole number), fillers (counts for the fillers found, in that order) and fillers_per_minute (to 1 decimal place).

Exercise10 points
Ready for questions

Write qa_prep(questions, answers): answers maps a keyword to a prepared answer. For each question, use the answer for the first keyword (in answers’ order) found in the question, ignoring case, and return "QUESTION -> ANSWER", or "QUESTION -> PREPARE" if none matches.

Exercise25 points
Challenge: ready to present?

Write talk_report(slides, plan, runs, transcript, seconds) with today’s rules (the helpers are given). Return (lines, verdict): "slides: ok" or "slides: N problems" followed by each problem indented by two spaces; then the lines of rehearsal_summary; then "pace: W words a minute, F fillers a minute". The talk is ready if there are no slide problems, every section’s median is within 10% of its plan, the total of medians is within the planned total, the pace is from 120 to 170 words a minute, and there are at most 4 fillers a minute. verdict is "ready to present" or "rehearse: " followed by, joined by ", ": slides; SECTION too long or SECTION too short for each section more than 10% off (in plan order); total too long; pace; fillers.

Your capstone today

  1. Plan your talk in the six sections and share out the minutes with allocate.
  2. Make the slides: a title on each, few words, a diagram for how it works, the numbers for results, notes for everything else.
  3. Rehearse three times, timed by section, and record one. Measure your pace and fillers from a transcript.
  4. Write answers to the five predictable questions, and give the talk to someone (a friend, a study group, a meetup) for real.
