Week 101, Day 4

Mentor session

Mentoring is teaching one person, over time, at their pace. Its tools are simple and hard to use well: questions that help someone think rather than questions that tell them the answer; listening more than talking; pairing where they hold the keyboard; and goals small and clear enough to finish by next week. Today you’ll classify questions, check goals, plan a pairing session, measure who did the talking in a real-looking transcript (session.txt, where the mentor’s best intentions take over), and write session notes, ending with a plan for several weeks of mentoring.

By the end of today you can

  • Ask open questions instead of closed or leading ones
  • Agree goals that are specific, measurable and dated
  • Pair with the learner at the keyboard, rotating roles
  • Listen more than you talk, and measure it
  • Keep notes that carry a mentoring relationship from week to week

Warm-up

The session started well: an open question, and the learner found the cause themselves. Then the mentor took over with a leading question and a long explanation, and the learner said “Okay.” That’s the commonest way mentoring goes wrong, and you can see it in the numbers.

Questions that help someone think

  • Open questions (“what do you think happens to a month with no walks?”) invite thinking, and are the mentor’s main tool.
  • Closed questions (“does it work?”) get yes or no; useful for checking, not for thinking.
  • Leading questions (“don’t you think a defaultdict would be better?”) are answers in disguise: the learner agrees without understanding.

When the learner is stuck, ask a smaller question rather than giving the answer. When they’ve found it, ask what they’d do next. Silence is fine: give them time.

Pairing and listening

Pair with the learner driving (at the keyboard) and you navigating (thinking ahead, asking): their hands, their learning. In longer sessions swap every ten minutes or so, so they also see how you’d think. Listen for what they don’t say: “Okay” after a long explanation usually means “I’m lost and too polite to say so”. A useful rule: the learner should talk at least as much as you.

Goals and notes

End each session with one or two goals for next time, small enough to finish: specific (what exactly), measurable (how you’ll both know), and dated (“by Friday: the monthly summary shows every month, with a test for an empty February”). Keep short notes: what you talked about, what’s done, what’s in the way, the goals, so next week starts where this one stopped.

Key idea

Ask open questions; avoid leading ones; give time. Pair with the learner at the keyboard and swap roles in longer sessions. Listen at least as much as you talk, and treat a quiet “okay” as a sign to stop explaining. Agree one or two specific, measurable, dated goals, and keep short notes from session to session.

Practice

Exercise10 points
What kind of question?

Write question_kind(text), ignoring case: "leading" if it contains don't you think, shouldn't you, isn't it, wouldn't it be better or why don't you just; otherwise "open" if it starts with what, how, why, which, where, tell me or walk me; otherwise "closed" if it ends with ?; otherwise "statement".

Exercise15 points
Who did the talking?

Write talk_share(transcript): lines start with a speaker and a colon ("Mentor: ..."). Return a dict from speaker to their share of the words spoken (not counting the speaker label), as a whole percentage, in order of first appearance, plus "longest": the speaker and word count of the longest single turn as "SPEAKER (N words)".

Exercise10 points
A goal you can finish

Write goal_problems(goal): report, in order, "say exactly what" if it has fewer than 6 words, "say how you'll know" if it has no digit and none of the words test, tests, passes, shows or prints, and "give a date" unless it contains by followed by a word (a day, a date or “next …”), ignoring case.

Exercise10 points
Taking turns at the keyboard

Write pairing_plan(learner, mentor, minutes, turn=10): blocks of turn minutes (the last may be shorter) with the learner driving first, alternating, as lines "START-END: DRIVER drives, NAVIGATOR navigates".

Exercise10 points
Notes for next week

Write session_notes(date, topics, done, blockers, goals): Markdown lines joined by newlines: ## Session DATE, a blank line, Talked about: A, B., Done: X; Y. (or Done: nothing yet.), In the way: Z. (or In the way: nothing.), a blank line, Goals:, and a - line per goal.

Bug hunt10 points
Counting turns, not words

Counting lines makes a one-word “Okay.” count as much as a sixty-word explanation, so this prints 50. Count words so it prints 70.

Bug hunt10 points
The mentor drove first

The learner should drive first, but the roles start the wrong way round and this prints 0-10: Ada drives. Swap them so it prints 0-10: Sam drives.

Daily challenge

Exercise25 points
Challenge: weeks of mentoring

Write mentoring_review(sessions): each session is a dict with date, goals (a list of goal texts set that day), met (how many of the previous session’s goals were met), blockers (a list) and transcript. Return lines: "sessions: N"; "goals met: M of G (P%)", counting each session’s met against the previous session’s number of goals (P whole, or - if there were none to meet); "recurring blockers: A, B" (in two or more sessions, compared ignoring case, in order of first appearance, or none); "mentor talk: A% to B%" (the mentor’s share in the first and last sessions, by talk_share); and "goals to rewrite: N" (goals with any goal_problems). Then a final line, "advice: listen more" if the last session’s mentor share is over 50, else "advice: keep going".

Your session

  1. Arrange a session with a learner (a friend starting Python, a study group, a beginners’ meetup): an hour, on their code.
  2. Pair with them driving, and keep a tally of your questions by kind.
  3. Record (with permission) or note who talked, and check your share afterwards.
  4. Agree a goal that passes goal_problems, write the notes, and arrange the next session.
