Week 97, Day 5

Behavioural interviews

Every interview loop has at least one conversation about how you work: “tell me about a time you disagreed with feedback”, “tell me about a failure”. These questions are predictable, and good answers are prepared, not improvised: a short true story with a clear structure, about what you did, ending in a result with a number in it. The good news is that this course has given you the stories: a sprint where you cut a feature, a review where you disagreed, a user test that changed your design, a first pull request to CPython. Today you’ll structure them with STAR, check them, match them to questions, and time them.

By the end of today you can

  • Structure answers as situation, task, action and result
  • Make the action about what you did, and the result measurable
  • Build a bank of stories that covers the common questions
  • Keep answers to about two minutes, without hedging
  • Prepare for follow-up questions

Warm-up

Three stories have detail and numbers; the fourth (outage) is what an unprepared answer sounds like: true, perhaps, but it tells the interviewer nothing about you. Today turns stories like the first three into answers, and stories like the fourth into ones worth telling.

What they’re asking

Behind each question is a competency: prioritising, handling feedback, communication, learning from failure, working with users, leadership. Interviewers want evidence from the past (“tell me about a time”), not promises (“I would…”). They listen for what you did (not “we”), how you thought about it, and what changed as a result. They also notice honesty: a failure story where you admit the mistake and show what you learned is stronger than a failure that was really someone else’s fault.

STAR

A reliable structure, kept to about two minutes (300 words at a speaking pace):

  • Situation: a sentence or two of context.
  • Task: what needed to happen, and your part in it.
  • Action: what you did, step by step, and why. This is most of the answer.
  • Result: what happened, with a number if you can (a time saved, a score that rose, a release that shipped), and what you learned.

Say “I” for your own actions. Cut hedging (“I think maybe”, “sort of”, “I guess”): it makes good work sound accidental.

A story bank

Prepare six to eight stories, each tagged with the competencies it shows, and practise them aloud. Most questions can be answered from a good bank: the sprint where you cut export answers “prioritising”, “deadlines” and “communication”. Spread the stories, so one interview doesn’t hear the same one three times. And expect follow-ups (“why did you choose that?”, “what would you do differently?”): a story with a clear reason and a lesson already answers them.

Key idea

Behavioural questions test competencies with evidence from the past. Answer with STAR: a little situation and task, mostly action (what you did, why, saying “I”), and a measurable result with a lesson, in about two minutes. Prepare a tagged bank of six to eight true stories, spread them across questions, and cut hedging. Expect follow-ups about reasons and lessons.

Practice

Exercise15 points
Checking a STAR answer

Write star_problems(story): story has situation, task, action and result (strings). Report, in order: "missing PART" for each empty part; "say more about what you did" if the action has fewer words than the situation, or fewer than 25 words; "say what you did, not only the team" if the action uses we (as a word, ignoring case) more often than I; "quantify the result" if the result has no digit; and "too long for two minutes" if the whole story has more than 300 words.

Exercise15 points
Matching stories to questions

Write match_stories(stories, questions): each question is (text, competency). For each question in order, choose a story tagged with that competency, preferring the one used least so far (ties by story order), and return (matches, unmatched): matches a list of (question text, story id), unmatched the question texts with no suitable story.

Exercise10 points
How long is it aloud?

Write speaking_time(text, wpm=150): the time to say the text’s words at wpm, rounded to the nearest second, as "M min S s", or "S s" under a minute.

Exercise10 points
Hedging

Write hedges(text): the hedging phrases found, ignoring case, as whole words or phrases, in this order, each once: i think maybe, sort of, kind of, i guess, probably, just, a bit.

Exercise10 points
Expecting the follow-up

Write follow_ups(story): likely follow-up questions, in this order: "Why did you choose that approach?" unless the action contains because, so that or why (ignoring case); "What would you do differently?" unless the result mentions learn, next time or differently; and "How did you measure that?" if the result has a digit but none of measured, score, rose, fell, from and to appears.

Bug hunt10 points
Counting I inside words

str.count("i") counts every letter i, in “it” and “fixed” too, so this prints 4. Count the word I alone so it prints 1.

Bug hunt10 points
Thirty seconds that vanished

Integer division drops the half minute, so 225 words prints 1 min. Show the seconds too, so it prints 1 min 30 s.

A mock round

Write six to eight stories of your own from this course and before it (your capstone, your open-source contributions, a group project, a job), tag them, and run them through prep_report with the questions in stories.py plus three of your own. Then answer three questions aloud to a friend, timed, and ask them what they remember a day later: that’s what an interviewer remembers too.
