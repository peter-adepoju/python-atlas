## What the exam looks like

The final exam is in two parts, on the first two days of next week. Both use the timer, have no hints or model answers on the page, and are checked like every exercise in the course: run your code and press **Check** as often as you like.

| Part | Day | What | Marks |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Day 1 | 15 quiz questions and 6 problems across the course | 105 |
| 2 | Day 2 | a larger program and a code review task | 75 |
| | | **Total** | **180** |

60% overall is a pass and 80% a distinction. Part 1 rewards breadth: every phase appears. Part 2 rewards putting things together: classes, data structures, error handling, tests and judgement in one piece of work.

## Planning your time

Work out a budget before you start, roughly in proportion to the marks, and keep 10% back for checking. If a problem takes twice its budget, move on and come back: an unanswered easy question costs more than an unfinished hard one.

:::exercise id=budget points=10 title="Your time budget"
Write `budget(parts, minutes, reserve=0.1)`: `parts` maps each part's name to its marks. Keep `reserve` of the time back for checking, share the rest in proportion to the marks (rounded down to whole minutes), and give any minutes left over by rounding to the reserve. Return a dict of each part's minutes, in order, plus `"checking"`.
@@starter
def budget(parts, minutes, reserve=0.1):
    ___
@@tests
def test_budget():
    "in proportion to the marks"
    parts = {"quiz": 15, "P1": 15, "P2": 15, "P3": 15, "P4": 15, "P5": 15, "P6": 15}
    plan = budget(parts, 150)
    assert plan == {"quiz": 19, "P1": 19, "P2": 19, "P3": 19, "P4": 19, "P5": 19, "P6": 19, "checking": 17}
    assert sum(plan.values()) == 150
    assert budget({"program": 50, "review": 25}, 120) == {"program": 72, "review": 36, "checking": 12}
@@solution
def budget(parts, minutes, reserve=0.1):
    usable = minutes - int(minutes * reserve)
    total = sum(parts.values())
    plan = {name: usable * marks // total for name, marks in parts.items()}
    plan["checking"] = minutes - sum(plan.values())
    return plan
:::

## When you're stuck

You will be stuck at some point; everyone is. Have a routine ready, the one you've practised all course:

1. **Reread the question**, slowly. Half of "stuck" is a misreading.
2. **Work a tiny example by hand** and write down what the answer should be.
3. **Write the simple version** first, even if it's slow: a correct slow answer scores; an unfinished clever one doesn't.
4. **Print** inside your code to see what's really happening.
5. **Move on** if it's still not working after your budget, and come back with fresh eyes.
